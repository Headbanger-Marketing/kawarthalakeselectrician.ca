Before work begins

Add up the equipment before you add any outlets.

Some homes keep every machine indoors; others have outbuildings, where a shop, shed or barn may hold the equipment that runs longest, like a welder, kiln, compressor or grow lights. Each of those buildings then needs its own picture of load and conditions.

Write down every machine or tool by the room where it runs, how many hours it runs and whether it is plugged in or hard-wired. Note any dust, damp or cold in that space, since it affects what equipment suits it.

What equipment details should I gather for a home workshop?

Collect the model plates or manuals for each machine, a list of what runs together and the hours it runs. Photograph each rating label as it sits, without moving the machine, so the contractor can read the details.

Does a detached shop need a separate feed or just more outlets?

That depends on the load and the run from the house, which the contractor must assess. Ask whether the proposal changes the supply to the building or only adds receptacles, and why it chose one over the other.

How can I compare quotes for a home business workspace?

Give each contractor the same equipment list and hours of use, then compare what each scope includes. A quote that leaves out a machine or a building is not describing the same job.

Who must agree before work starts on a home business space?

Confirm who can authorize the work, then check whether the municipality needs a building permit for any structural change. The contractor handles the ESA notification, and an insurer may also want to know about commercial equipment.

Does sending the form hire anyone or confirm a contractor visit?

No: sending it hires nobody. This site is a source of planning information and reviews the enquiries it collects; no visit, price or electrical work is decided here. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor separately confirms who it is, its licence and the job's scope, and agrees cost and dates with you; check that ECRA/ESA licence first.
