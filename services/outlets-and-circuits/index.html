An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Kawartha Lakes property

Record the appliance model information, where the plug is now and what else shares that wall or outlet, such as a bench tool, a shop vacuum or a charger. Note whether the space is damp or unheated.

Ask whether the appliance needs its own circuit, whether the receptacle should be a type suited to a damp or unfinished space and how the contractor will label the result for you.

What should the assessment establish?

In a Kawartha Lakes garage or basement, a freezer or second fridge is plugged in. The circuit it uses now is identified, along with everything else connected to it. Hobby tools and shop equipment on the same wall are listed, so the proposal reflects what can run together. The contractor weighs the damp or cold in that space and names any receptacle type that suits it. Whether the appliance warrants a dedicated circuit, and why, appears in the scope, which also shows how any new circuit is marked on the panel directory.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a chest freezer in the garage need its own circuit?

The contractor should decide that after reviewing the appliance information, its location and what else shares the circuit. Ask for the answer and the reasoning in the written scope before work is agreed.

Can I run a freezer from an extension cord in a basement?

Extension cords are a poor match for equipment that runs continuously, and the contractor can explain a safer arrangement. Ask about a receptacle close to the appliance instead of a longer cord.
