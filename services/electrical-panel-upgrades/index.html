Panel and service upgrades in Kawartha Lakes.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Kawartha Lakes property

Stand a few steps back and photograph the panel door with the space around it, then write down what is stored or built nearby. Sketch where the nearest bench, shelf or machine stands, since that may need to move.

Ask whether the work needs a clear area in front of the panel, who is expected to move the stored items and whether the proposal covers any rebuilt shelving or wall finish afterward.

What should the assessment establish?

Can a hard-to-reach panel stay where it is? The contractor's conclusion is recorded, with the reasons the location is awkward. How much clear space does the work need around it? That clearance is stated, and whatever must be moved to provide it is named. Will the incoming supply change, or only the panel? The quote names any change beyond the panel itself and whether the utility needs to be involved. Where home business or hobby equipment adds load at a Kawartha Lakes property, each item is listed with how it was considered.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a panel stay where it is if shelving blocks the front?

That is a question for the contractor, who must judge clearance and access. Ask what the proposal says about the existing position, and whether moving the panel would add a separate scope of work.

Which shop details matter when the panel sits near my machines?

Name the machines that run in that area and how long each is in use. Mention sawdust, moisture or stored materials near the panel, because the assessment should reflect the actual conditions.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
